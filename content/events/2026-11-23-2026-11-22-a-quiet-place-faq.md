# A Quiet Place: Frequently Asked Questions

*A Quiet Place* is a private journal designed to keep you company in grief. It is simple to use, whenever you want, in whatever way you want. There is nothing you have to finish.

---

## What is A Quiet Place?

It is a simple place to write about your grief after someone you love has died. You can come when you like and leave when you need to. You can also give it to someone else as a gift.

Inside you will find:

- **Today's Check-In:** tap the feelings, thoughts, and body sensations that are true today, and skip the rest.
- **Write:** open space to write, with gentle prompts if you would like one.
- **The Four Questions:** four questions about who you are now, and who the person you love is to you now. The answers will not come all at once, and that is okay.
- **Remember Someone:** a keepsake page for the person you miss, for their sayings, memories, and what they taught you.
- **Learning About Grief:** short, plain-language topics, including grief and the body, sudden loss, losing a child (of any age), grieving before a death, and how grief differs from depression.
- **Reaching Out:** crisis lines, signs it may be time to talk with someone, and where to find support.
- **My Journal:** everything you have written, in one place, ready to print or save as a PDF.

After you save something, the journal asks where you would like to go next. Pages also end with a row of buttons for moving around.

## Who made it?

It draws on more than 50 years of supportive care by a Licensed Professional Counselor (Barbara Davis) and a health care chaplain (Michael Davis). It is based in part on our book, *Healing Passage: Charting paths for your course through grief*.

## Is it therapy?

No. *A Quiet Place* is **not** a substitute for counseling, psychotherapy, or care from a licensed health care provider, and it is not emergency help. Links to a number of resources are included in the journal. We make no representations about its fitness, accuracy, or ability to help with the grief process. We offer it in the hope that it will fit you and your experience.

If you are in crisis or thinking of ending your life, call or text **988** (U.S.) at any hour. If you are in immediate danger, call 911. Outside the U.S., please contact your local emergency number or crisis line.

## Getting started

### How do I open my journal and unlock it?

Most people open it as a web page. The email from us has the link and your license code.

1. On a laptop or desktop computer, click the link in the email, or type it into your web browser.
2. On the Welcome page, click the line that says **"I have a license code: unlock my journal."**
3. Type your name **exactly** as it is written in the email.
4. Copy and paste the **whole** license code.
5. Press **Unlock**.

You only need to do this once in each browser. After that, the journal remembers.

### What is the "I have a license code" box at the top of the Welcome page?

You can try *A Quiet Place* for free. The preview lets you save up to **3 entries** to see whether it suits you. The box at the top of the Welcome page explains this and shows how many you have used.

- **If you do not have a code yet,** there is nothing you need to do about the box. Look around and write whenever you are ready.
- **If you have a code,** open the box and enter your name and code, as described above.

After 3 entries, the journal asks for your code before it saves more. Your words are safe. You can still save a backup, read Learning About Grief, and use Reaching Out.

### Which browser should I use? Can I use it on my phone?

*A Quiet Place* is designed for a **laptop or desktop computer**, and that is on purpose. Grief deserves your full attention, and phones don't invite that. It is something to sit down with, with a cup of coffee or tea, when you want and as you want. We have not tested it on phones or tablets, so please don't rely on it there.

On a laptop or desktop, **Firefox, Chrome, Safari, Opera, and Edge** work well.

### Is it an app?

No. It is a web page, so there is nothing to install. You can also keep a copy as a single file on your own computer (see below).

## Where your words are kept

### Where are my words saved?

In the browser you used, on that computer. Nothing is sent anywhere. That means:

- Please use **the same device, the same browser, and the same link** each time you come back.
- A different browser or a different computer will show an empty journal until you restore a backup.
- Clearing your browser's data would erase the journal. That is why backups matter.

### My journal looks empty. What happened?

Your writing is still there if it was saved. The most common reasons for an empty journal are:

- You opened it in a **different browser** than before (for example, Chrome instead of Firefox).
- You are on a **different computer**.
- You used a **different link**. Even adding "www." to the web address, or opening the saved file instead of the web page, shows a separate journal.
- You were in a **private or incognito window**, which forgets everything when it is closed.
- Your browser's data was **cleared**.

First, try going back to the browser, computer, and link you used before. If that does not bring it back, restore your most recent backup (see below). Because your writing is never sent to us, we are not able to recover it for you.

### What is a backup, and how do I make one?

A **backup** is a copy of everything you have written, saved as a small file. Think of it as a photocopy of your journal, kept in a safe drawer.

Your journal lives only in your browser, on your computer. If the browser's data is cleared, or the computer is replaced or breaks, the writing would be gone. A backup file lets you bring it back, or move it to another computer.

**To save a backup:**

1. Open **Settings** (top right of any page).
2. Find the card called **Save a backup** and press **Save a backup**.
3. A small file with a name like `grief_journal_backup_2026-11-22.json` goes to your **Downloads** folder (or wherever your browser keeps downloads).
4. Open your Downloads folder and move or copy that file somewhere safe: your Documents folder, a USB stick, or a cloud folder you already use.

A good habit is to save a backup once a month, or any time you have written something you would hate to lose. The journal will remind you when it has been a while. Each backup holds everything, so you only need to keep the newest one. If you have set a password, your backup is locked with the same password.

### How do I restore a backup?

Use this on a new computer, in a new browser, or if your journal looks empty.

1. Open the journal in the browser you want to use, and unlock it with your name and code if it asks.
2. Open **Settings** and press **Restore from a backup**.
3. Choose your backup file. If it is locked with a password, you will be asked for it.

Restoring only **adds what is missing**. It never erases or overwrites what is already in the journal, so it is safe to try. If everything in the backup is already there, the journal tells you that nothing needed to be added.

### Can I keep a copy of the journal on my own computer instead of the web page?

Yes. Some people prefer to keep the journal as a file on their own computer, for example if they have concerns about privacy or would rather not depend on a website. The email from us includes the journal file as an attachment.

1. Save the attached file somewhere you will remember, such as your Documents folder.
2. Double-click it. It should open in your web browser. If it does not, right-click the file (Control-click on a Mac), choose **Open with**, and pick Firefox, Chrome, Edge, or Opera.
3. Unlock it the same way: click "I have a license code," then enter your name and code.

Good to know:

- **The file and the web page keep separate journals.** Please choose one to write in.
- Open the file the same way each time, and please don't move it. Your writing is tied to where the file is kept.
- **Safari** may not allow the password or license features from a saved file. If you use Safari, use the web page, or open the file in another browser.
- To move your writing from the web page to the file, press **Save a backup** on the web page, then open the file and press **Restore from a backup**.

### Do I need an account or an internet connection?

No account is needed. To open the web page you need an internet connection. After that, everything you write is kept on your own computer, not on the internet. If you use the saved file, you do not need an internet connection at all.

### Is my writing private?

Yes. Nothing you write is sent to us or to anyone else, and no one else can read it. The web page delivers the journal to your browser. It does not collect what you write. (Like any website, it can tell that the page was opened, but never what is in your journal.) The only exception is if you choose the optional Google Calendar reminder (see below).

### Can I protect it with a password?

Yes. In **Settings**, you can set a password. Your words are scrambled on your computer, so they cannot be read without it. Backups made after you set a password use the same password.

**Please write your password somewhere safe.** If you forget it, no one can recover your journal, including us.

### How much can I write? What is the storage meter?

Your browser gives the journal roughly 5 MB of room, which is space for a great many entries. In **Settings**, the "How full is my journal?" meter shows how much you have used. When it passes about 60%, the journal suggests a backup. When it is almost completely full, please save a backup, print older entries to PDF, and delete the ones you no longer need. If space ever runs out, your words stay on the page, and nothing is lost until you close it.

## Using the journal

### Can I add photos?

No. *A Quiet Place* is not a photo gallery. Your photo service or phone will keep and organize pictures far better than we can. You can write about a photograph in a keepsake page, and that is often just as moving.

### Can I print or share what I wrote?

Yes. In **My Journal**, tick the entries you want and choose **Print / Save as PDF**. You can also add a summary of your check-ins to bring to a doctor or counselor. Each entry has a **Copy to share** button, and nothing leaves the journal unless you choose it.

### Can it remind me of anniversaries and birthdays?

Yes, in a gentle way. On a keepsake page with a date of death or birth, the journal notices when the day is near. You can also add a **yearly reminder to your own calendar**. Choose a calendar file (it stays on your computer, and works with most calendar programs) or a Google Calendar link. The Google option opens Google Calendar in a new tab, and Google will see the title. If you prefer, you can leave the person's name out of the calendar entry. The Google Calendar link turns on when you enter your license code.

### I'm not religious. Is this still for me?

Yes. On the Welcome page you choose what place, if any, faith or spirituality will have: **plain** (no spiritual language), **awe, beauty, and meaning**, or **faith and prayer**. You can change it at any time in Settings.

### Is it written for every kind of loss?

It is written for grief after a death. The keepsake page is open enough that you can remember a pet too. Many people find parts of it helpful after other kinds of loss, but it was not designed for them.

## License, gifts, and the story behind it

### How does the license work?

You can save a few entries in the free preview to see whether it suits you. To keep writing, you enter your name and the license code that came with your purchase. The code works only with the name it was made for. That name appears at the top of the journal ("Name's Journal") and on every PDF, so please keep your code private. In Settings, under **Whose journal is this?**, you can change the name shown at the top, and add a line in memory of someone, such as "In loving memory of Dad." It then appears at the top of every page and on anything you print, and the license name stays in the footer and on printouts.

### Can I give it as a gift?

Yes. When you order, give the name of the person the journal is for, exactly as it should appear. That name becomes the owner's name on the journal and on every PDF.

### How was this made? What role did AI play?

We want to be open about this. The words and ideas in *A Quiet Place* come from our own writing, including our book *Healing Passage: Charting paths for your course through grief*, and from years of caring for grieving people. The Four Questions are inspired by Thomas Attig's book *How We Grieve: Relearning the World*. Other sources are listed under "Credits and sources" in Settings.

Neither of us is a programmer. We used Claude, an AI assistant from Anthropic, to help turn that material into a working program, and we could not have built this tool without that help. Anthropic is not affiliated with this journal and has not reviewed or endorsed it.

The program is deliberately simple and private. It works as a web page or as one file on your own computer. It has no account, collects nothing, and sends nothing to us or to anyone else. The only exception is the optional Google Calendar reminder, which opens Google Calendar only if you click it. Keeping it simple was a choice, not a limitation.
