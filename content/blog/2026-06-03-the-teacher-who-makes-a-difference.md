**Point** - Have you ever discovered some new and amazing facts in your adult life that you missed in your previous education? The past several weeks, my wife and I've been watched histories of some of the American Presidents. It's been fascinating! My wife said, *I've learned more about (choose any of them: Abraham Lincoln, George Washington, or Franklin Delano Roosevelt) in the past two weeks than I learned in my whole life*.

Sometimes, I'm captured by big ideas watching or listening to podcasts with any of the following --- mathematician Hannah Fry, *A Way with Words* hosts Martha Barnett and Grant Barrett, or physics with either Michio Kaku or Jim Al-Khalili. My wife and I played a game a while back: *Who was one teacher who really engaged you in their subject?* There was a follow-up question: *What made their approach different or sticky?" In every case, the teacher cared about engaging students and about making their "subject matter" really *matter*. They *pushed themselves* to find tools so their students could *see* connections with daily life. When I watch one of their videos or hear their podcasts, I think, *I wish I could have had Fry, Kaku, Al-Khalili as a teacher. I might have made them regret their profession. But, I don't think so! I think they have found ways to make ideas matter. 

That task, making things matter, is ours too. We're teaching people all the time; our children, grand-children, younger colleagues at work, older colleagues, or friends. First, we are teaching about caring. Do we care enough to help people see the connections with life. Second, are we willing to prove that our subject matters?

A psychiatrist friend of ours always said, *Your problem is that you care.* The only way to teach the next generation is to care as much about what and how were teaching as we are about the checkboxes. 

**Counterpoint** - I know that what I wrote isn't the whole truth. In fact, I have lots of great teachers in the family. So, I also know something else is true. By design, our educational (and healthcare, mental health, and other civic systems) eke away the passion that a new person possesses. BY design, our educational systems are designed for compliance and preparation for industry, not life.
